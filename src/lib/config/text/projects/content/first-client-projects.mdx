## Overview

**Stack:** Next.js · TypeScript · Strapi · Resend · Docker · Dokploy · PHP · Git

New Art Vanguard and Arsenale Moto were my first paid client contracts, both obtained through referrals during my final year of high school.

The projects required **two different kinds of ownership**. [New Art Vanguard](https://www.newartvanguard.com/) was an existing production website that needed careful corrections, new pages, responsive improvements, and a more repeatable development workflow. [Arsenale Moto](https://arsenalemoto.it/) had no website, so I designed and delivered the complete system from scratch: public pages, a role-based CMS, a customer valuation flow, and the production infrastructure behind them.

_The main lesson was that client delivery begins before implementation._ A client describes the result they need; the developer must turn that description into scope, interfaces, permissions, deployment decisions, and a system that remains usable after handoff.

</ProjectMdxRevealSection>

## Goal

The goal was not simply to publish two websites. It was to leave each client with **a practical way to continue operating after launch**.

For New Art Vanguard, that meant improving a live codebase without introducing unnecessary disruption. For Arsenale Moto, it meant replacing social-media-only motorcycle publishing and developer-dependent page edits with a consistent website that staff could manage through a CMS.

## Technical Approach

I adapted the engineering approach to the constraints of each project rather than applying the same process twice.

### New Art Vanguard

The original request was to correct text and images across the existing site and create new pages. Within a three-developer team, I delivered changes end to end while coordinating through Git and preserving the site's existing behavior.

I updated **more than 5 existing pages and built 3 new pages**. The most distinctive addition was an animated coming-soon page, which required fitting a new visual experience into an older codebase without making the rest of the site feel disconnected.

The main difficulty was the combination of inconsistent legacy code and responsive behavior. Instead of rewriting the application, I worked within its existing structure, reused patterns where possible, and introduced cleaner responsive sections where the old implementation was fragile.

I also helped standardize development with Docker. The resulting workflow reduced repeated setup and release preparation from roughly 20 minutes to 15 minutes, a **25% improvement**, while making the project less dependent on any one developer's machine.

### Arsenale Moto

Arsenale Moto needed a new operational workflow, not only a public-facing design. Before the project, motorcycles could be promoted through social media, while a website listing would otherwise require someone to edit source code manually.

I built **6 public pages** with Next.js and TypeScript and modeled **more than 30 motorcycle listings** in `Strapi`. Each listing exposes over 10 editable fields for information such as pricing, specifications, imagery, and page content. Staff now fill in the CMS fields, and every motorcycle page is generated with the same structure and visual style.

The CMS supports **10 distinct roles**. I translated the responsibilities of different staff members into permissions so that routine content work remained accessible while sensitive actions stayed restricted.

I also connected the valuation form to `Resend` with server-side validation, anti-spam controls, authenticated domains, and branded email templates. In production, it handles approximately **10–20 customer valuation requests per day**.

## Delivery Model

The two contracts demonstrate **opposite sides of client engineering**.

New Art Vanguard required controlled contribution inside a shared system. I had to understand an unfamiliar codebase, coordinate with two teammates, and improve it without turning every inconsistency into a rewrite.

Arsenale Moto required full ownership. A push to GitHub triggered deployment through Dokploy, while I configured the VPS, Docker services, domains, `HTTPS`, environment variables, backups, and health checks.

The handoff included CMS training, documentation, deployment instructions, credential management, and ongoing support. The client received more than a finished website: they received a workflow for publishing motorcycles, receiving leads, deploying updates, and maintaining the system.

</ProjectMdxRevealSection>

## Challenges

The hardest problem was **translating business responsibilities into a CMS that non-technical staff could use safely**.

The client did not arrive with a content schema or permission matrix. I had to understand which information changed frequently, which fields belonged together, how a motorcycle should move from draft content to a complete listing, and which actions should remain limited to specific roles.

This required asking practical questions rather than technical ones. Instead of discussing database fields, I focused on what each employee needed to accomplish and how much time they had to do it. Those answers became the content model, editing interface, and role permissions.

The result was a publishing process that takes roughly **one minute**: an authorized employee fills in the relevant CMS fields, and the website produces a consistent listing without direct source-code changes.

## What I Learned

At school, the task is usually defined in advance, and a teacher is available to clarify what the expected result should be. Real client work is different: the client often has limited time, explains the need in business terms, and expects the developer to identify the missing technical decisions.

_I learned that requirements are not something I simply receive._ They are something I help produce by asking precise questions, making assumptions visible, and confirming what success means before implementation moves too far.

I am most proud of the Arsenale Moto CMS because it turned that translation work into a practical system. _The value was not the CMS technology itself._ It was giving ten different roles a fast, controlled way to manage content without depending on a developer.

</ProjectMdxRevealSection>

## Future Improvements

The main improvement I would make in future client work is to **document requirements more precisely** before implementation.

I would summarize the agreed pages, editable content, responsibilities, approval criteria, and unresolved questions in one concise requirements document. This would give both sides a shared reference, reduce ambiguity in message-based feedback, and make later changes easier to evaluate against the original scope.
